# Flexible Intelligent Metasurfaces for High-Mobility ISAC: Hardware Evidence, Adaptation Timescales, and Validation Gaps

---

## Abstract

A flexible intelligent metasurface (FIM) treats the physical shape of an aperture as a control variable alongside its electromagnetic state. Some recent system studies place that variable inside high-mobility integrated sensing and communication, where the delay–Doppler channel varies rapidly, and argue that shape adaptation follows it. This review asks whether existing hardware supports that argument: which operations in a FIM-assisted high-mobility link plausibly require fast adaptation, which may run on slower geometric or statistical timescales, and what has been measured about either?

We separate the architecture classes the FIM evidence base draws on, decompose FIM adaptation into ten stages from sensing to stabilised radiation, and record every timing value with its timed object, start and end events, and measurement status. No reviewed platform measures the duration of more than four of the ten stages, and the platform that reaches four has no radio-frequency layer. The two best-covered platforms do not overlap where it matters: one commands its shape but does not radiate, and the other radiates but cannot command its shape. No independently measured settling or post-deformation calibration duration is reported in the reviewed set, and stabilised radiation is timed only with geometry held static. Tracing the hardware-feasibility premises of the FIM system literature back to their primary sources shows element-level response times presented as surface morphing periods, and a tabulated value with no counterpart in the cited source.

The contribution is the audit and the reporting discipline it motivates; we propose no hardware and do not claim the architecture is infeasible.
