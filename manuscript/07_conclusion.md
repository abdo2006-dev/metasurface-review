## 7. Conclusion

This review asked which operations in a FIM-assisted high-mobility ISAC system must track fast channel variation, which may follow slower geometric or statistical change, and how well existing hardware supports the resulting timescales. The stage-resolved answer is that, conditionally on the two-timescale premise the system literature itself supplies, the fast path comprises channel and pilot acquisition — the S1-C branch of the sensing stage — channel estimation, the optimisation that produces the fast state, the transport of that command to the surface, and the electronic state update; the geometry stages of geometry sensing (the S1-G branch), geometry estimation, morphing, settling and geometry-dependent calibration need not track instantaneous fading under that premise, and stabilised radiation remains the outcome of the chain rather than a member of either group. The evidence behind those two groups is asymmetric, and in the opposite direction to the usual presentation: the demand on the fast path is established by analysis and simulation, while the hardware capability on it is measured — but measured in fragments, on the externally deformed flexible aperture of A3 and on the rigid panels of A5, neither of which commands its own geometry.

The central validation gap is the intersection rather than the ingredients. Large flexible apertures radiate and remain programmable while bent; surface shape can be sensed in situ; electronic state updates on a semiconductor panel have been timed at the controller; commanded mechanical shape control has been demonstrated with closed-loop verification. Each was demonstrated on a platform lacking one of the things a FIM needs together: a radiating layer, a geometry the system commands, and a channel it observes. Within the reviewed set the best-covered platforms hold three of the four components that assumption requires — a radiating aperture, shape sensing, channel estimation and optimisation — and the platforms that command their geometry hold fewer, because commanded actuation is not one of the four. No platform holds all four. So no reviewed source times the adaptation chain end to end on one object, none measures the duration of more than four of the ten stages, and the platform reaching four does not radiate. Four intervals are unmeasured: commanded actuation of a radiating aperture, settling of one to an electromagnetically stated tolerance, post-deformation calibration duration, and the onset of trustworthy radiation after a commanded geometry change.

The contribution is the audit and the reporting discipline it motivates: a taxonomy stating which measurements may transfer between physically different platforms, a decomposition stating which process a number times, a register recording the definitions, a traceability result bounded to the cases in which it was verified, and a reporting set derived from the observed deficiencies. Three substitutions are unsafe, each demonstrated by a value in the register and not merely asserted: an element-level figure is not an aperture-level one; a partial electronic compensation loop is not an adaptation latency; and an interval ending in stabilised radiation, measured with the geometry held static, is not a measurement of stabilisation after a commanded morph. We make no novelty claim for the two-timescale or statistical-CSI principle, which is established in the movable-antenna and FIM literatures, and we propose no new hardware or control algorithm.

Among the gaps identified, timing the recalibration experiment that has already been performed appears to require the least new hardware: post-deformation closed-loop recalibration of a large flexible aperture has been demonstrated and not timed. Synchronised timing from a curvature change to a re-converged focus, at a stated performance tolerance, would convert that demonstration into a quantitative constraint, provided the apparatus remains available and the two events can be timestamped against a common clock. Alongside it, reporting the object, the start event and the end event of every published interval would have prevented every substitution documented in §5.2.

Two limits bound all of this. The corpus was assembled by convenience rather than by protocol, so absence of a measurement in the literature we read is not evidence that the measurement does not exist, still less that the capability is unattainable; every absence stated here is bounded to the reviewed set or to the documented searches. The traceability finding is bounded twice over, by that set and by full-text access to 13 of the 20 candidate studies. Whether flexible intelligent metasurfaces work in high mobility remains open. The narrower claim made here is that in the cases we traced, the confidence expressed in the feasibility premises exceeds the evidence behind them.
