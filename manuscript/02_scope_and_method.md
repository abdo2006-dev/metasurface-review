## 2. Review scope and method

This is a structured critical review. It is not a systematic review, a systematic mapping study or a scoping review, and it does not adopt PRISMA terminology, because it does not satisfy the requirement those labels carry: a reproducible, protocol-driven, multi-database *search*. What it does possess is a reproducible *extraction* protocol, and the two are distinguished here rather than blurred.

**Corpus.** The reviewed set comprises 24 distinct research contributions across 31 files. They are *distinct* rather than *independent*: several share authors, and duplicate versions, conference-and-journal pairs and same-group design lineages were collapsed into independence groups so that related publications are never counted as mutual confirmation of one another. Four of the files are supplementary-information or peer-review documents belonging to two of the papers; they are evidence *about* those papers rather than evidence alongside them. Three sources were readable only as page images, and no load-bearing claim is drawn from them.

The corpus was assembled by convenience rather than by a protocol-driven database query, so inclusion criteria are stated retrospectively and describe what the set *is* rather than a prospective filter: peer-reviewed articles, preprints and author manuscripts concerning FIM system modelling; flexible or conformal reflective and transmissive metasurface hardware; programmable mechanical surface actuation; electronically reconfigurable RIS hardware reporting timing or power; high-mobility channel estimation for reconfigurable surfaces; and reviews of any of these. Sources with no wireless, electromagnetic or mechanical-actuation content were excluded. Six sources were first read as preprints or author manuscripts; publisher-deposited records confirm versions of record for five, and the published full texts of the three carrying the most weight were obtained and inspected. Every load-bearing timing and parameter quantity keyed to them survives at the published locators, which this manuscript cites. One non-timing claim did not survive that check, and the affected statement is not used here (Supplementary Section S3).

**External searches.** Two searches supplement the corpus, and their evidential weight differs. A documented but non-systematic external search (ten queries, 17 August 2026) established whether a FIM-specific review exists, whether two-timescale or statistical-CSI FIM control is already published, whether any measured FIM actuation latency exists, and where the hardware-feasibility timing claims in the FIM system literature originate. It used a general web-search interface, which does not expose reproducible hit counts, so its results are recorded as search outcomes rather than as established absences.

A forward citation search (18 August 2026) is reproducible, and its two stages are reproducible in different senses. Stage one is automatic and deterministic for the dated search: every work citing the three primary hardware sources was retrieved through the OpenAlex citation graph, giving 306 records and 262 unique works after deduplication by identifier, of which 65 passed a title-and-abstract screen against an explicit wireless and communications expression. Stage two is manual and recorded rather than re-derived: excluding optics, photonics, materials, mechanics and imaging work, and collapsing versions under the independence rule below, leaves **20 distinct flexible-metasurface or reconfigurable-surface system studies**, of which 13 were read in full and 7 are behind publisher paywalls with no open deposit located. No access control was circumvented, and every count arising from this search is a lower bound. The seed identifiers, the query, the deduplication rule, the screening expression, the per-work decision table and the retrieved records as they stood on 18 August 2026 are available as runnable code and data in the accompanying public reproducibility repository;³ re-running the code against the preserved records reproduces the retrieval and this screen exactly, while a live re-query may return a different set, because OpenAlex is updated continuously. Supplementary Section S5 gives the full flow and the study-family register.

**Extraction.** Extraction, unlike search, followed a fixed protocol applied uniformly to every source. Each source is assigned to an architecture class with explicit transfer rules (Section 3); each source's treatment of each of the ten adaptation stages (Section 3) is coded; and every reported timing value is entered in a register carrying its timed object, a named start event, a named end event, a measured/simulated/projected/derived flag, the architecture on which it was obtained, and a comparability class. Two values may be compared only if their comparability classes intersect.

Four principles govern the coding, and each exists because a specific confusion is possible without it. The complete symbolic scheme, the seven-level evidence ladder used to grade evidence from analytical derivation through prototype measurement to end-to-end closed-loop validation, and the per-source coded matrix are in Supplementary Section S6.

1. **What a source establishes about a stage and what it establishes about the stage's duration are separate questions.** A measured radiation pattern is a quantitative result and not a timing; a fully delimited *simulated* runtime is a timing and not a measurement. Both are routine in this literature, and a single code would misread both.
2. **An accuracy is never timing evidence.** A root-mean-square deviation, a displacement error or any other accuracy figure remains an accuracy, however quantitative it is. We also distinguish a measured stage duration from duration information available only as a stated share, component or residual of a larger aggregate; from a rate; and from an interval the experimenter deliberately allocated. Only the first licenses the verb *times*.
3. **Evidence is never upgraded.** A system simulation is not hardware validation, a measured actuator is not a radiating system, an electronic switching time is not a mechanical settling time, and a protocol unit — a subframe, a time slot, an iteration count — is not a duration, since none of the sources reporting them supplies the numerology needed to convert.
4. **Independence is tested, not assumed.** A preprint and its version of record are two versions of one study; a conference paper and its journal extension are one lineage; supplementary material belongs to its parent paper; and independence between citing works is tested by author-set intersection rather than inferred from differing first authors.

**Absence claims.** Every statement about what is missing is reduced to one of two checkable forms: *this named document does not report quantity Q*, or *across the N sources in this named table, no row records Q*. No statement about the field as a whole is made anywhere in this manuscript. Absence of a measurement in the literature we read is not evidence that the measurement does not exist, still less that the capability is unattainable.

One further constraint is inherited by everything downstream. No reviewed source reports a repetition count or a dispersion figure for its headline response, morphing or adaptation interval, so none of the values on which this review's arguments turn carries an uncertainty. One subordinate process quantity does carry a dispersion — a function-evaluation cycle stated as 0.35 ± 0.15 s — so the deficiency is specific to the headline intervals rather than universal. It is a deficiency in the sources rather than in the extraction, and additional literature cannot retroactively supply uncertainty for values already published without it.
