## D. Load-bearing claims (verbatim from `manuscript/01_introduction.md` §1.6)

The manuscript's four contributions, quoted rather than paraphrased so their evidence framing is not accidentally strengthened in the retelling:

> **C1 — A stage-resolved adaptation-chain decomposition specific to FIMs.** Ten stages from sensing to stabilised radiation, with each source's treatment of each stage classified as demonstrated, measured, simulated, assumed, absent or unclear. No reviewed source measures more than five of the ten stages; the largest linked set on a single platform covers S1–S2 and S4–S6, with channel estimation absent; no source measures channel estimation together with a commanded shape change; and the stages that carry measurements are distributed across four physically non-equivalent architectures whose timing quantities are not interchangeable.
>
> **C2 — A timing register with explicit definitions, and the two simplifications it qualifies.** Recording start and end events shows that an element-level update time cannot be substituted for a tile- or aperture-level configuration time — the same panel reports both, up to two orders of magnitude apart — and that the ranges reported for electronic and for mechanical processes overlap once the tuning mechanism is named, a liquid-crystal switch-off exceeding a measured multi-stage electronic compensation chain on a flexible surface.
>
> **C3 — A traceability audit of the hardware-feasibility premises used by the FIM system papers examined here.** Three premises are traced to their primary sources, and the primary measurement is compared with the value in circulation. A forward citation search of the three primary hardware sources — 262 citing records screened — finds the substitution in four papers, and finds no author-disjoint publication group reproducing it: every instance shares an author with the others. It also finds papers citing the same hardware and attaching no timing value at all. This is therefore a documented finding about one collaboration network, not a measurement of the field, and no prevalence claim is made.
>
> **C4 — A reporting framework** in which each field is motivated by a recurring reporting deficiency or by a specific unresolved quantity identified in the reviewed set, and which prescribes definitions rather than acceptance thresholds.

**Evidence classification: C3 is held at Level A** in `documentation/novelty_boundary.md` and `documentation/evidence_strength_matrix.md` — a documented pattern within one traced collaboration network, on **zero author-disjoint confirming groups** out of seven system papers still unread behind paywalls. If a later, better-resourced search finds an author-disjoint instance, the classification moves to Level B; that is the single open item that could change a contribution's wording. Reviewers should read `manuscript/08_traceability.md` and `documentation/audits/independence_audit.md` directly rather than relying on this summary for anything load-bearing.

## E. Known limitations (verbatim summary from `documentation/draft_status.md` §3)

- **No domain-expert review has occurred.** The largest single gap; this project cannot close it by itself.
- **No interval in the manuscript carries an uncertainty.** No reviewed source reports a repetition count or dispersion figure for its headline timing value.
- **Seven identified system papers are behind publisher paywalls and were not read.** Every count from the forward-citation search is a lower bound; an author-disjoint instance among them would move C3 from Level A to Level B.
- **Some sources are identified bibliographically only.** Quantitative claims keyed to them should be re-verified against published versions before submission — marked in bold in `manuscript/99_references.md`.
